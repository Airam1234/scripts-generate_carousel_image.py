# House style

Plain British English, written for someone reading on a phone between shifts.

## Phrases to avoid

These read as machine-written and have been struck from previous issues:

- "shining a light on" / "shines a spotlight on"
- "the numbers speak for themselves"
- "a real testament to their resilience"
- "you've got this"
- "we're excited to announce"
- "dive into" / "unpack"
- "journey" used as a metaphor for a course
- "empower", "unlock", "elevate", "leverage"

Replace with a plain statement of the thing. "Mental Health Awareness Week runs from 11 to 17 May" beats "we're shining a light on mental health this May".

## Punctuation and mechanics

- **Em-dashes**: avoid as a default connector. A full stop or a comma usually works. One per page is plenty.
- **Exclamation marks**: at most one per issue, and only in genuinely celebratory copy.
- **Spelling**: British throughout — organise, recognise, programme (for a course), enrolment, apologise.
- **Numbers**: spell out one to nine in prose, numerals from 10. Always numerals for ages, percentages, money and dates.
- **Dates**: "5 August 2026", not "August 5th" or "05/08/26".
- **Time**: 24-hour or "9am to 5pm", consistently within an issue.

## Tone

The brand tone of voice asks for: straightforward and clear, knowledgeable but easy to understand, addressing the reader as an equal rather than talking down.

In practice:

- Say "you" and "we", not "learners are reminded that".
- Give the instruction before the rationale. "Tell us the same day. Early concerns are easier to resolve" reads better than the reverse.
- Where something is uncertain, say so plainly rather than hedging with corporate vagueness.

## Content constraints that keep coming up

- **No "no fails" claims.** Do not state or imply a 100% pass rate.
- **Real governance figures only.** No estimates, no rounded-up placeholders presented as fact.
- **Prevent stays in.** The Prevent duty section is retained in safeguarding content; do not drop it to save space.
- **Functional Skills eligibility stays general.** 2025 policy varies by learner age and apprenticeship type, so write "check with your coach" rather than stating who is exempt.
- **Crisis resources must be current.** Direct eating disorder support to the National Alliance for Eating Disorders, not NEDA, whose helpline is permanently disconnected. Check any helpline still operates before printing its number.

## Safeguarding copy

Where the newsletter tells someone what to do about a concern, be concrete and short. Name the person, give the address, give the phone number, and say what happens next. Reassurance is useful ("raising a concern does not put the apprenticeship at risk") but it goes after the instruction, not instead of it.

Always include the 999 line for immediate danger.
